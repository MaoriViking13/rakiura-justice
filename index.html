The Crown's Errors

The Legislative Breaches

How the government legally stole the rights from the specific descendants and opened them to a generalized list, diluting the ownership and violating the 1864 Deed.

1. Native Land Claims Adjustment Act 1910

Act No. 75

The Crime: The Inquiry that ignored the Appendage.

This Act empowered the Native Land Court to determine "beneficial owners." Instead of looking at the 1864 Appendage (the contract), the Court began constructing a new list based on general "Rakiura" ancestry, ignoring the specific island allocations made by the Chiefs.

Impact: Opened the door for non-descendants of the specific island owners to claim rights they were never gifted.

2. Reserves and Other Lands Disposal Act 1921

Section 64

The Crime: Cementing the Error.

This Act finalized the lists created by the 1910 inquiry. It turned administrative errors into statutory law. It created the "Beneficial Owners" list which stands in direct contradiction to the "Appendage Owners."

Impact: Thousands of legitimate descendants were disenfranchised, and thousands of illegitimate claimants were added. This is the basis of our current Section 45 application.
